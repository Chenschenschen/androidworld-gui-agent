prompt tokens sent, per turnHow a conversation worksTurn 1: you send your question.Turn 2: your question, the answer, and the new question.Turn 3: all of that, plus the new one.Every turn, the entire history is transmitted again, from scratch.•Cost and latency grow as a conversation goes on. •Eventually you hit the contextwindow, and something has to be dropped or summarised. •Memory is not a property of the model. === PAGE 22 ===
THE CONTEXT WINDOWWhat falls out of the windowTruncateDrop the oldest turns.+ Cheap.− The agent forgets what youdropped.
SummariseReplace the old part with a compressed version.+ Keeps the themainpoint.− Quietly loses the detail you did not think mattered —an order number, a promise.
RetrieveKeep everything outside; pull back what is relevant now.+ Scales.− New failure mode: it brings back the wrong thing and the model confidently uses it.•Nothing here is free. All of the three throws something away. The design question is “what am I willing to lose, and what happens when I lose it?”•Models do not use a long context evenly. Material in the middle of a very long window is attended to less reliably than material at the start or the end. A bigger window is not a better memory.

=== PAGE 28 ===
FAILURE MODESWhat it cannot do by itself
?ActIt cannot send an email, query a database, or tap a screen. Anything that happens in the world happens because a program outside read its output and did something.
?RememberNo state between calls. Anything that looks like memory is actually just the process of the system putting text back into the window.
?VerifyThere is no truth signal in next-token prediction. It cannot tell you whether what it just produced is correct.
Three gaps. Every one of them is filled by something outside the model —and that “something outside” is what the rest of this course is about.

=== PAGE 37 ===
FROM MODEL TO AGENTThree ways the loop breaksThe loop does not endThe model keeps calling tools, or calls the same one with the same arguments over and over. It is stuck: the observation it needs is not arriving, or it arrived and was not recognised.
Limit the number of iterations.
The tool is called wronglyRight tool, wrong arguments —or the wrong tool entirely
Fixed by better descriptions and stricter schemas, not by a better system prompt. 
It says it is done when it is notIt reports success without having verified anything.
The most dangerous of the three, because it is silent. The other two show up in your logs; this one looks like success.
None of the three is fixed by a smarter model. They are fixed by the loop, the tool definitions, and the check at the end.
