ONE TRAINING STEPGRPO, PPO, DPO: whom do you compare against?GRPOGroup Relative Policy OptimizationCompare each attempt with its siblings on the same question. No separate critic.
PPOProximal Policy OptimizationA second model estimates the expected reward at each step, and every action is compared against that estimate. 
DPODirect Preference OptimizationNo attempts at all. Offline pairs of (chosen, rejected) answers; push the model towards the chosen one. Cheap, and only as good as the pairs.
Algorithmhow attempts are comparedRewardwhat counts as successEnvironment and datawhich problems the model ever meets

=== PAGE 28 ===
SFT VS RLA Tendency, Not a LawWhy SFT droppedA limited demonstration set reinforced a literal constant. When the constant changed, the memorised mapping was applied anyway.
Why RL roseReward reinforced a procedure—recompute until the total is right. Procedures transfer where constants do not.
•Diverse, well-regularised SFT can generalise perfectly well.•Situations:•There are many possible solutions, but it is easier to verify success than to demonstrate the ideal solution.•Amplifiessuccessful behavioursthat already exists at low probability. It cannot create ability the model never had.

TRAINING AN AGENTThe Recipe1Build or borrow the environmentThe tools the agent may call, a way to reset to a known state, and enough copies to run dozens of attempts in parallel.
2Define the rewardOutcome as the baseline. Penalties for forbidden actions on top. Both computed from the environment, never from the agent’s own report.3Roll out, score, compare, updateAnd mask the tokens the environment returned: they were not produced by the policy. Leave them in and the model learns to predict its own sandbox.
